## Policy versions and the cancel-and-retry attack

**Measured, synthetic** (test days 42–59, the same frozen model hgb-86it-677lab-nocallerid for every version; each version's alert lines are calibrated for it on the same validation week and budgets; only the policy rules differ). The cost to genuine customers who cancel is **modelled**.

- **v1** is Round 1's policy.
- **v2** adds retry memory: after a warning the customer didn't go ahead with (cancelled, called the bank, trusted person declined, or no answer), a payment to a new payee, or to the cancelled one, within 24 hours is at least a pause.
- **v3** adds two rules. A first payment to a payee with an outside flag (cybercrime registry, DoT high risk, or a live mule network) is at least a pause. And memory raises a payment to a trusted check only if it is ₹50,000+ or the payee is flagged; otherwise to a pause.
- **v4** (the finale) adds a journey-only pause line: 30% of the pause budget goes to the journey family's own score, so a scam-like journey is paused even when the payee looks clean and the phone signals are silent (the fused line keeps the other 70%, so the total budget is the same). It also drops caller identity, which a bank app can't read: the caller's DoT rating no longer counts as context evidence.

## Design choices: what was tried

**Measured, synthetic** (test days 42–59; model hgb-86it-677lab-nocallerid, policy v4; lines from the validation week each time). These are the two model-side choices made for the finale, kept here so they can be rerun.

**A. Adversarial training: tried and rejected.** Attacked copies of the training-day scams were added to the fused model's training data.

|  | Victims warned | At first payment | False pauses /10k | Chat-only | Split | Aged mules | Clean mules | All at once |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| + all four attacked copies | 94.2% | 67.8% | 0.92 | 94.3% | 96.8% | 85.8% | 100.0% | 100.0% |

With the clean-mule copies in training the clean-mule and combined attacks look solved (100% and 100%). That is the model learning the edit's fixed values (an account exactly 900 days old, idle exactly 2 days), not the scam: with those copies held out, clean mules move only from 79.4% to 80.3%. Inside the decision service a decision takes 10.8 ms at the median (15.8 ms p99): models, policy, reasons, message and evidence record. With more requests at once the service queues them (one Python process); a bank would run several processes behind a load balancer, so the per-request time, not this laptop's throughput, is the number that carries over. The bank server handles one judge case at a time (it keeps every session's history in memory).

Failure paths are tested in `tests/test_failures.py`: the payee feeds time out (decided without them), the decision service is down (the bank's own rules apply after a 0.5 s timeout, nothing held), no officer acts within 30 minutes (the hold is cancelled and the money stays), a call-back can't reach the customer (nothing is cancelled), and no consent (the phone sends nothing). ## Limits

- **All Thehro numbers are synthetic.** They come from our own simulator (seed 7), which is cleaner than reality.
  Only a shadow-mode pilot on real payments can confirm them.
- **Scams are 158–315× more common in the synthetic world than in reality,** so there are enough to measure.
  Precision at India's real fraud rate is therefore *estimated*, not measured.
- **Money protected is *modelled*:** it depends on assumed stop rates (`config.STOP_PROB`) that a pilot must measure.
- **PaySim is account takeover, not coached scams.** It tests the method (point-in-time features, gradient-boosted
  trees, budgeted alert lines), not the scam signals: it has no calls, no coaching and no cross-bank mule networks.
- **Attacks change the scammer's behaviour, not the genuine customers'.** A real adversary would also adapt over
  time; the attacks are one round, with the model frozen.
